Three stones that lie when dry

From the free field guide. Tap each stone to wet it. The drawings are code-drawn stand-ins, not photos.

  • Lake Superior agate

    Dry
    A dull, waxy, often pitted red-brown pebble that hides in plain sight.
    Wet
    Glossy and a little see-through at the edges, sometimes with thin bands on a broken face.

  • Petoskey stone

    Dry
    A plain gray or tan limestone pebble, maybe faintly mottled.
    Wet
    Tightly packed six-sided cells jump out, each with a dark eye in the center.

  • Leland Blue

    Dry
    A dull blue-gray lump.
    Wet
    Strong blues, purples, grays, and greens. Often swirled, with gas bubbles.

    Not a natural stone: it is slag from a 19th-century iron furnace in Leland, Michigan.
