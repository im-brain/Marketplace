# Plan: Great Lakes Rockhounding Store

## Context

Brian wants a print-on-demand store for Great Lakes rockhounds (tees and socks, made in the US by Printify) that earns more than his roughly $20/month AI bill. Tools must stay under $75/month. He is new to coding, so the store should run itself except for the steps where his judgment matters. The profit model lives at `models/POD_Profit_Model.xlsx`. Sourcing is domestic only for now, so the overseas model is out of scope.

Decisions from this conversation: **Shopify + Printify**. **Product-only social content**, with no face on camera. **Art will mix his own photos, public photos, and AI art**, with guardrails below. **Budget: AI about $20, tools under $75.**

When Brian approves this plan, the first build step writes these files to the repo:
- `PLAN.md`: this plan, in plain language.
- `CLAUDE.md`: the handoff file. It records the current step, decisions, account status, where the secrets live, and his preferences (no em dashes, Oxford comma, .xlsx and .docx for anything he edits, dark-mode HTML). It is updated at the end of every work session.
- `PLAN.docx`: a Word copy, because he cannot open .md files on his work computer.

## Pushback (built into the plan)

1. **"Publicly gathered photos" can't go on products.** A photo found online belongs to whoever took it. We only print his own photos, his own edits, art made with code, or images whose license clearly allows commercial use (logged in the tracker). Public photos are fine as references for checking accuracy, but they are never traced or printed. AI art is allowed, but each piece needs his own edits so it can be protected.
2. **Product-only video reaches the fewest people.** One cheap upgrade without showing his face: film his real stone held next to the shirt that was drawn from it. The point of the brand is that every design starts with a real find.
3. **Launch 4 designs, not 13.** The tracker holds all 13, and only 4 go live first.
4. **The saturated puns ("I lick rocks" and "Sorry I'm late") come later.** The launch set should be designs nobody else has, which means Great Lakes details and accurate charts.
5. **"Bands will make her dance" is close to a Juicy J song title.** A reworded version is fine, and "Agate bands make her dance" reads clearly as a rock joke. Check the trademark for apparel (Class 25) and music (Class 9 and Class 41) before printing.

## The concept: "Wet Stone"

Every rockhound wets a stone to see its true color. That is the brand idea, the interaction on the site, and the voice. Working names to check: **Wet Stone Supply Co.**, **Third Coast Rockhound**, and **Lake Effect Rock Co.** Brian picks one in Step 3 after domain and trademark checks.

What makes it something new:
- **The "wet" effect.** Product photos and stone photos show dry and dull. Hovering or tapping "wets" them: the color deepens and a gloss sweeps across, the way it does on the beach. This is the site's signature, and it is light CSS and JavaScript added to a Shopify theme.
- **Every design has a field note.** The stone it came from, the beach it was found on, and a one-line story. Rockhounds trust provenance more than polish.
- **A free field guide on the site.** The ID charts we print on tees also live on the site as a free interactive guide to Lake Superior agates and beach stones. People search for this, it builds trust, and every chart links to its shirt.
- **Socks drawn by code.** The Petoskey hexagon pattern (a Voronoi cell pattern) and the agate bands are generated by a script. The art is original, any size is easy, and new colorways cost nothing.

## How it runs itself

```
Brian adds a design (PNG or SVG) + fills a tracker row
        |
   [Agent] checks file size/DPI, guardrails, trademark status field
        |
   [Agent] creates Printify product (variants, prices from profit model)
        |
   [Agent] writes title, description, tags, field note (no em dashes, Oxford comma, no unverifiable claims)
        |
   [Agent] pushes to Shopify as a DRAFT
        |
   >>> BRIAN: approve in Shopify (one click) <<<
        |
   Customer orders -> Printify prints & ships automatically
        |
   [Weekly scheduled agent] sales + profit report, next week's 3 video captions, reminders
```

**Brian's only manual steps (the human gates):** confirming each chart is accurate, the trademark check, approving the art, approving samples, publishing a draft, and filming and posting the weekly batch of videos (about 1 hour a week).

## Steps (each one testable on its own)

**Phase 0: Foundation**
1. Create `PLAN.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, and `PLAN.docx`, and commit them. Add the profit model to `models/`. *Test:* Brian opens PLAN.docx.
2. Create the accounts: Shopify (free trial), Printify, and a Printify API token stored as an environment secret, never in the repo. *Test:* Brian can log in to both, and Claude can read the Printify catalog with the token.

**Phase 1: Brand**
3. Shortlist names, then check domains and the USPTO for each. Brian picks one and buys the domain. *Test:* the domain is owned.
4. Brand kit: colors (the lake palette: Superior slate, agate red, Petoskey gray, and sand), fonts, a simple wordmark, and a one-page brand guide (.docx). *Test:* Brian approves it.

**Phase 2: Designs**
5. Design tracker `.xlsx` with all 13 designs, plus columns for art source and license, accuracy check, trademark check, and launch priority. It follows his spreadsheet formatting rules. *Test:* it opens in Excel and Brian marks the launch 4.
6. Proposed launch 4: the Great Lakes beach stone chart, "Agate Expectations," "Will brake for agates," and Petoskey hexagon socks.
7. The code-drawn Petoskey sock pattern, made to Printify's exact sock template size. *Test:* Brian views the PNG and it passes Printify's upload preview.
8. The other three designs at print size, all from Brian's photos, his edits, or properly licensed sources. *Test:* Brian approves each one, and the accuracy and trademark boxes are checked.

**Phase 3: First product by hand (to learn the flow)**
9. Brian and Claude create one product in Printify together through the website and publish it to Shopify. *Test:* the product appears in the Shopify admin.
10. Order samples of the launch 4. *Test:* samples arrive and Brian approves print quality and color.

**Phase 4: The beautiful store**
11. Install a free Shopify theme and apply the brand kit. *Test:* the store preview looks on-brand on phone and desktop.
12. Add the "wet stone" effect as a custom section. *Test:* hovering or tapping a product makes the color bloom, on phone and desktop, in light and dark mode.
13. Field notes on product pages (Shopify metafields). *Test:* each product shows its stone, beach, and story.
14. Field guide pages (agates and beach stones), linking to the shirts. *Test:* Brian checks every label for accuracy.
15. About, shipping, and returns pages, plus policies. *Test:* Shopify's launch checklist has no missing items.

**Phase 5: Automation**
16. Script `publish_design.py`: takes a tracker row, creates the Printify product, and publishes it to Shopify as a draft. *Test:* run it on one design, then check the draft in Shopify.
17. Listing writer plus a checker that blocks em dashes, missing Oxford commas, and flagged claims. *Test:* a draft listing passes the checker, and Brian reads it.
18. Weekly scheduled task: an orders and profit summary, 3 product-only video ideas and captions, and reminders, such as samples to order and trademarks to re-check. *Test:* the first report arrives on schedule.

**Phase 6: Launch**
19. Remove the store password and publish the launch 4. Post the first video batch. *Test:* place a real order and watch it reach Printify.
20. After 30 days: review the numbers, decide on designs 5 to 8, and decide whether to add Etsy.
