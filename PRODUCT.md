## Platform

web

## Stack

Phase 1 to 3 concept: plain static HTML, CSS, and a little vanilla JS in `site/prototype/`, with no framework, so it ports cleanly. Production: a custom Shopify Online Store 2.0 Liquid theme, hosted by Shopify on the Basic plan and editable in the theme editor. Field Guide profiles and Field Notes are planned as Shopify metaobjects. Hydrogen was considered and set aside because of its extra code, hosting, and deploy upkeep (confirmed by Brian, Nov 2, 2026; re-check against current shopify.dev docs before Phase 4).

## Users

Great Lakes rockhounds: hobbyists who hunt Lake Superior, Lake Michigan, and inland lake beaches in Michigan, Wisconsin, and Minnesota. They know a Petoskey stone from an agate, and some hunt Leland Blue or UV-reactive sodalite. They own field guides, belong to ID groups, and notice mislabeled stones right away. Most arrive on phones from TikTok and Instagram product videos. The job: buy a tee (or socks as an add-on or gift) that shows they belong to this hobby, without wading through generic nature merch.

## Product Purpose

Heavy Pockets Co. (heavypocketsco.com) sells print-on-demand graphic tees and socks for rockhounds. Shopify handles the catalog, checkout, and orders, and Printify prints and ships them. Success means steady tee sales from organic social traffic, enough profit to pass the owner's roughly $20 monthly AI bill, and a free Field Guide good enough that people share it.

## Positioning

Apparel made by an actual Great Lakes rockhound, with accurate stone knowledge and insider jokes, built around the idea that dry rocks lie (confirmed Nov 2, 2026).

## Operating Context

- The rockhound's ritual: walk the shoreline head down, pick up a dull stone, wet it to see its true color and banding, and fill your pockets.
- The source of the brand idea, "Wet Stone": every rockhound wets a stone to see its true color.
- Visitors land on a product or the homepage from short product-only videos (no faces).
- Owner: Brian, a rockhound in Grand Rapids, Michigan. He is new to web development and maintains content through Shopify's admin.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- The Shopify store doesn't exist yet. It opens at PLAN.md Step 12, after samples are approved and the preview is signed off. Nothing is live.
- Launch catalog (concepts, not yet products): Great Lakes beach stone chart tee, "Agate Expectations," "Will brake for agates," Petoskey hexagon socks, and "I lick rocks" (a dull, dry stone with one glossy, lick-shaped streak). No prices, variants, availability, or product photos exist yet, so don't invent them.
- Tees drive profit. Socks are add-ons and bundle items.
- Signature features: the Wet Stone interaction, a field note on each design, a free Field Guide (6 to 10 curated stone profiles at launch, browsable, not an identification service), and a small Field Notes journal.
- Future, not at launch: an interactive ID helper. Never claim definitive identification.
- Email signup: subtle, with no pop-ups or fake urgency. The provider is undecided until Shopify exists.
- Brand colors and fonts are provisional until the approved web direction becomes the Step 4 brand kit.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Heavy Pockets Co. Taglines: "Head down. Pockets full." and "Dry rocks lie."
- Voice: confident, casual, and authentic. Clever and understated about 80% of the time, and weird and funny about 20%. No pun overload, generic adventure language, or corporate polish.
- Copy rules: always use the Oxford comma, and never use em dashes.
- Brand references Brian named as binding inspiration (principles, not templates): D.S. & DURGA, Collected, BAGGU, Brain Dead, Patagonia (a favorite), FILA Explore, and Godly-style sites. Nostalgia order: 1990s, modern eclectic, 1970s outdoors, and 1980s playfulness.
- It must not read as a stock Shopify theme, a POD or Etsy novelty shop, a geology museum, a vintage field journal, a beige-and-sage outdoor template, or a generic AI site.
- No protected IP: no logos, teams, characters, lyrics, or band art. Slogans are checked on USPTO before printing.

## Product Principles

1. Shop first. Story supports the sale and never hides price, size, or the cart.
2. Be right. Every stone name, location, and fact is checked by Brian before it goes live. Use SAMPLE labels until then.
3. Dry rocks lie. Calm and plain at first, rich when you engage. The reveal is earned, not constant.
4. Show the real product. Never make garment images more saturated or colorful than the real print.
5. Built for phones first, because that's where people arrive.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA as the target. Readable light and dark modes, keyboard and screen-reader support for the menu and Wet Stone, honoring reduced motion with a non-animated fallback, and comfortable touch targets.
